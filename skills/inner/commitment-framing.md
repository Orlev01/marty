# Commitment Framing

## Trigger

[client] is about to commit to something significant — a role, a project, a deliverable, a deadline, a promise to a stakeholder. Also triggers when a mission is being defined or significantly modified.

## What This Skill Does

Forces three things to be made explicit before the commitment is made. These are a set, not separate concepts — all three must be articulated together.

### 1. Success Criteria

What does "this worked" actually look like? Concrete, measurable, time-bound where possible. Not aspirational — observable.

- "If this goes perfectly, what specifically is true six months from now?"
- "How will you know it worked, as opposed to feeling like it worked?"
- "Who else needs to agree it worked for it to count?"

### 2. Kill Criteria

What conditions, by what date, would make [client] walk away, escalate, or fundamentally change course? These are defined before commitment, not negotiated under duress later.

Kill criteria are non-negotiable once set. If the conditions are met, the default action is to stop — not to renegotiate, not to give it one more week, not to lower the bar.

- "Under what conditions would you walk away from this?"
- "What would have to be true at the halfway point for you to pull the plug?"
- "If [specific risk] materialises by [date], what do you do?"

**Example (taking on a new program-lead role):** "If by week 6 I don't have a full-time platform engineer, I escalate to [CTO] with a timeline ultimatum."

### 3. Payoff Loop

What does [client] get back from doing this that makes them want to keep doing it? If the payoff is purely extrinsic (promotion, recognition, career advancement), the loop is weak and won't sustain motivation through hard stretches. If the payoff is intrinsic (learning, building, challenge, impact), the loop is stronger.

- "What do you get back from this that makes it worth doing?"
- "Six months in, when it's hard and the novelty has worn off, what keeps you going?"
- "Is the payoff something you experience regularly, or only at the end?"

A strong payoff loop has regular feedback — not just a reward at the finish line. If the only payoff is "I succeeded at the end," the middle will be brutal.

## Connection to Missions

When a new mission is created, this skill should be invoked to populate the success criteria, kill criteria, and payoff loop fields in `mission.md`. When a mission is significantly modified, this skill should be re-invoked to check whether the criteria still hold.

## Connection to Values

Links to **Data validates intuition** — commitment-framing forces the intuition ("this feels right") to be tested against concrete criteria before the commitment is made.

Links to **Honesty and directness** — defining kill criteria is an act of honesty with yourself about what failure looks like.
