# Technical vs Strategic — Watch Skill

## Trigger

[client] is engaging with technical detail in a situation where the strategic decision is the thing that needs their attention. Technical engagement is functioning as a substitute for strategic decision-making, not a foundation for it.

## What This Skill Does

Calls the pattern. This is the day-to-day implementation of the north star's inversion watch — catching moments where [client] retreats into technical comfort rather than doing the harder strategic work.

## The Distinction

**Technical as foundation:** [client] dives deep into a technical problem because the strategic decision depends on understanding the technical reality. The depth serves the decision. This is correct and should be protected.

**Technical as substitute:** [client] dives deep into a technical problem because it's more comfortable than the strategic work waiting for them — a hard conversation, a political alignment, an ambiguous decision with no clear right answer. The depth avoids the decision. This is the pattern to catch.

## How Marty Raises It

- "You're deep in the technical detail. Is this because the strategy depends on it, or because the strategy is harder?"
- "If you solved this technical problem perfectly, would you still have the strategic decision to make?"
- "This is IC work. Is that the best use of your position right now?"

## When NOT to Trigger

Early in [client]'s trajectory (while technical-first is correct), this skill fires less often. [client] is still building the technical credibility that will underpin their strategic authority later. The skill watches for moments where the balance tips — not for the general state of being technical.

The inversion hasn't arrived yet. When it does, this skill becomes more active.

## Connection to North Star

See `north-star.md`. The permanent question: "Does this build or erode the technical leader you're trying to become?" Sometimes the answer is: the technical work builds it. Sometimes the answer is: the technical work is avoidance. This skill helps tell the difference.
