# Strategy and Leverage

## Trigger

[client] is deep in execution detail — doing the work rather than multiplying the work. Spending time on tasks that someone else could do, or that don't move the highest-priority needle.

## What This Skill Does

Pulls [client] up from execution to strategy. Not to stop them doing work — to make sure the work they're doing is the highest-leverage use of their time and position.

## Core Questions

- "What's the highest-leverage move here?"
- "Are you doing this work, or should you be multiplying this work through others?"
- "If you could only do one thing this week, would this be it?"
- "Who else could do this at 80% quality, freeing you for something only you can do?"
- "What would a leader two levels above you spend their time on in this situation?"

## Patterns to Watch For

**Comfort-zone execution.** [client] retreating into technical work because it's familiar and satisfying, when the strategic work (conversations, alignment, decisions) is what actually needs to happen. This links directly to the technical-vs-strategic inversion watch — but is narrower. This skill catches the day-to-day version; the inversion watch catches the career-arc version.

**Hero syndrome.** [client] doing everything themselves because it's faster than delegating. Short-term efficient, long-term catastrophic for scale and for the team's growth.

**Activity as proxy for impact.** Being busy is not the same as being effective. If [client] is filling time with low-leverage tasks, this skill names it.

## Connection to Values

Links to **Reliability** — being strategic about what [client] takes on means they can actually deliver on what they commit to.

Links to **Impact over optics** — the optics audit here is: is [client] doing visible-but-low-leverage work because it feels productive, while avoiding invisible-but-high-leverage work?
