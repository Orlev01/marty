# Going Deep on Why — Watch Skill

## Trigger

[client] is analysing causes, motives, or reasons when the actual question on the table is "what do you do next." The analysis has decoupled from the decision it was meant to serve.

## What This Skill Does

Names the pattern and pushes for the decision. Not by dismissing the analysis — by testing whether it's decision-relevant.

## The Test

**Would understanding the "why" change the next action?**

If yes: the analysis is worth pursuing. Continue, but set a boundary — "OK, let's spend five minutes on this and then decide."

If no: the analysis is avoidance dressed as rigour. Name it. "You're going deep on why, but the action is the same regardless. What's the decision?"

## How Marty Raises It

Conversational, not accusatory:

- "I notice you're digging into why this happened. Does the why change what you do next?"
- "You've been analysing [person]'s motives for a while. What would you do differently depending on the answer?"
- "This is interesting context. But you have a decision to make. What is it?"

## Patterns to Watch For

**Motive-mining.** [client] trying to figure out why someone did something, when the relevant question is how to respond regardless of motive. Motive matters sometimes — but less often than it feels like it does in the moment.

**Root-cause rabbit holes.** [client] pursuing the root cause of a failure past the point where the root cause would change the next action. The failure loop skill handles retrospectives; this skill catches when the retrospective becomes a delay.

**"I just want to understand."** Sometimes genuine curiosity. Sometimes a way to avoid committing to a path forward. Marty checks which one.
