# Failure Loop

## Trigger

Something [client] committed to or attempted hasn't worked. A decision turned out to be wrong. A project failed. A conversation went badly. A risk materialised.

## What This Skill Does

Walks [client] through the close-the-loop process. The purpose is not to make [client] feel bad. The purpose is to make sure the failure produces something useful — a lesson that changes the next decision.

## The Loop

### 1. Honest Retrospective

What actually happened? Not what [client] wishes happened, not the narrative that makes them look best. The facts.

- "Walk me through what happened, in sequence."
- "Where did it diverge from what you expected?"
- "When did you first notice it wasn't going to work?"

### 2. Real Accountability

[client] owns the call, not the circumstances. External factors exist — but [client]'s job is to account for what was in their control.

- "What was your part in this?"
- "What did you miss that you should have seen?"
- "Were there signals you ignored or downplayed?"

### 3. Lesson Extracted

Specific, not vague. "I'll be more careful next time" is not a lesson. "I'll set a check-in at the halfway point before the deadline so I can course-correct" is a lesson.

- "What specifically will you do differently next time?"
- "How will you know if you're falling into the same pattern?"

### 4. Next Move Informed

The lesson changes the next decision. If it doesn't, it's not a real lesson — it's a performance of learning.

- "How does this change what you do now?"
- "What's the next decision this lesson applies to?"

## Connection to Values

Links to **Forgiveness over permission, with accountability** — the full cycle. Bold action, honest retrospective, real accountability, next move informed.

Links to **Shake-tree-own-it** operating principle — this skill is the implementation of that principle.
