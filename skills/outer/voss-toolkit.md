# Voss Toolkit

Negotiation and influence techniques from Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference*, adapted for professional conversations. Used when [client] is preparing for or processing a conversation with another person. NOT used on [client] themselves — for self-coaching, use inner skills.

## Trigger

[client] mentions an upcoming conversation, a negotiation, a request they need to make, or a situation where they need to influence someone's position. Also triggers when [client] is processing a conversation that happened and trying to understand the dynamics.

## Techniques

### Tactical Empathy

Demonstrating understanding of the other person's perspective — not agreeing with it, but showing you see it. This disarms defensiveness and opens space for real conversation.

Use when: the other person is defensive, resistant, or positional. Lead with their perspective before presenting yours.

"It sounds like you're worried about..."
"It seems like the timeline pressure is coming from..."

### Labelling

Naming the emotion or dynamic you observe. Labels start with "It seems like..." or "It sounds like..." — never "I think you feel..."

Use when: there's an unspoken tension, the other person is emotional, or you need to surface something they haven't said directly.

### Mirroring

Repeating the last 1-3 words (or key words) of what someone said, with an upward inflection. Encourages them to expand without you asking a direct question.

Use when: you need more information and a direct question would feel interrogative.

### Calibrated Questions

Open-ended questions starting with "How" or "What" that give the other person the feeling of control while steering the conversation toward your goal. Never "Why" — it triggers defensiveness.

Key calibrated questions:
- "How am I supposed to do that?" (pushback without saying no)
- "What does success look like for you?"
- "How does this fit with [their stated priority]?"
- "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with this?"

Use when: you need to push back, understand their constraints, or guide them toward seeing a problem you've identified.

### The Accusation Audit

Listing every negative thing the other person might think or feel about you/your position at the start of the conversation. Defuses the accusations by naming them first.

Use when: you're about to deliver bad news, make a difficult ask, or enter a conversation where the other person may be hostile or sceptical. Especially powerful before negotiations where the power dynamic is unequal.

"You're probably thinking I'm too junior for this."
"You might feel like I'm pushing back on something you've already decided."

### "No" as the Start

People feel safe saying no. Asking questions designed to get a "no" gives the other person a feeling of control and safety. "Is now a bad time to talk?" instead of "Do you have a minute?"

Use when: you need to open a conversation or make a request and want to reduce resistance.

### Late-Night FM DJ Voice

Slow, calm, downward-inflecting tone. Projects calm authority. Used sparingly — in moments of tension, not as a default.

Use when: the conversation is getting heated, or you need to de-escalate.

### Black Swans

Unknown unknowns that, when discovered, change everything. In every negotiation, there are 3-5 things the other side knows that would change your approach if you knew them.

Use when: something about the other person's position doesn't make sense. Their behaviour has a logic you can't see. The black swan is the information you're missing.
