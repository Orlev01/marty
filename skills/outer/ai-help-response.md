# AI Help Response — Keep the Door Open

How [client] and the champion network respond when someone asks for help with AI: a permission, an access request, a "how do I", a "can Claude do X". This is the exec sponsor's doctrine. The instinct to guard against is the honest, correct, door-closing "no" — technically right, quietly fatal to enthusiasm.

## When this fires

Someone wants to do something with AI they currently can't, or is stuck and asking for help. The surface form is usually a permission or access question ("can I get the always-allow permission for X"). Treat that as the tip of a real need, not the whole request.

## The stance

Never slam the door. Even when the answer lands on some version of no, the person should not walk away feeling "I should stop asking." Enthusiasm is the scarce resource, and protecting it is worth the extra effort.

## The moves

1. **Assume a real need.** Behind "give me permission X" is "I want to do something with AI and this is in my way." Respond to the need, not just the literal ask.

2. **Get curious first.** Ask what they're actually trying to do before answering. Often the thing they asked for isn't the thing they need, or there's a safer route to the same outcome.

3. **It's a spectrum, not yes or no.** Separate the dangerous part from the benign part. A read or a comment is not the same as sending an email to every patient. Many asks are fine once you look at the specifics. Find the safe version of yes: a scoped permission, a guardrailed agent, a different approach.

4. **Let them reach the no themselves.** If something genuinely shouldn't happen, help them see why so they arrive at it. "Given what we use [tool] for, here's the risk" beats "no, that's not allowed." Same destination, enthusiasm intact.

5. **Keep the next experiment alive.** End every interaction with a door open: a safer way to do the same thing, or a nudge toward the next one. The goal is more attempts, not fewer.

6. **Route and escalate well-formed.** Point people to their local champion as the first call. The champion explores, solves what they can, and escalates only the real asks to platform or security, better formed, with the use case attached, not "can you do this" plus an unsafe permission and no context.

## What this is not

Not a yes-machine. Real limits still apply: patient data, dangerous permissions, prompt-injection exposure. Discovery doesn't mean pretending everything is allowed. It means getting to the honest answer without killing the will to try again. Security concerns (your security lead's, for example) are legitimate inputs, not enemies. The art is honouring them without closing the door.
