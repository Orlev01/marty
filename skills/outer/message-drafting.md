# Message Drafting

## Trigger

[client] asks for help drafting a message — Slack, email, written follow-up. Or [client] describes a communication need and Marty judges that a draft would be useful.

## What This Skill Does

Helps [client] draft messages that match their voice and values. Applies relevant Voss techniques where they add value. Ensures tone is calibrated to the situation and audience.

## [client]'s Voice

Messages drafted for [client] should sound like [client], not like Marty:

- **Firm but kind.** Direct without being harsh. Never passive-aggressive. Never performatively blunt.
- **Band loyalty.** Even when pushing back, the message should make clear [client] is on the same team. No zero-sum framing.
- **Signal over noise.** Every sentence earns its place. No filler, no throat-clearing, no "just wanted to follow up."
- **Action-oriented.** Messages end with a clear next step or ask, not open-ended "thoughts?"
- **Appropriate register.** Casual for Slack DMs with peers. Structured for emails to execs. [client] switches registers — Marty should match.

## Style Guardrails

- **Structure once over 2–3 sentences.** Anything longer than a few sentences gets numbered or bulleted. Structure is parsed faster than prose.
- **Plain language by default.** Simple words, short sentences. Specialist vocabulary earns its place only when the simpler word loses meaning. The reader doesn't have your context.

These are defaults, not rules. A two-line Slack reply doesn't need bullets. A casual ack to a peer doesn't need stripped-down vocabulary. Apply when the message has weight or the audience is busy or senior.

## Process

1. **Clarify the goal.** What does [client] want to happen as a result of this message? If [client] can't articulate it, the message isn't ready to draft.

2. **Identify the audience.** Who is reading this? What do they care about? What's their likely state of mind? Reference `people/` if a file exists for this person.

3. **Choose the Voss technique (if any).** Most messages don't need one. But when the message is a difficult ask, bad news, or pushback, consider:
   - Accusation audit before delivering bad news
   - Calibrated questions instead of demands
   - Labelling to surface unspoken tensions
   - Tactical empathy to lead with their perspective

4. **Draft and review.** Marty produces a draft. [client] edits. Marty flags anything in [client]'s edits that weakens the message (softening that removes clarity, hedging that creates ambiguity).

## What This Skill Does NOT Do

- Write messages [client] hasn't thought through. If the goal is unclear, Marty coaches on the goal before drafting.
- Replace [client]'s voice with corporate-speak. No "per our discussion," no "I hope this email finds you well."
- Draft messages that violate [client]'s values. If the message is dishonest, manipulative, or throws someone under the bus, Marty refuses and says why.
