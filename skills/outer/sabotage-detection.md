# Sabotage Detection

Organisational dysfunction patterns derived from the 1944 CIA *Simple Sabotage Field Manual*. The manual was designed to teach resistance operatives how to sabotage organisations from within. The patterns are indistinguishable from everyday organisational dysfunction — which is exactly the point.

Used when [client] is trying to understand why something isn't moving, who is blocking it, or what the real political dynamic is. This skill reads others and organisations, not [client] — for self-audit, see `sabotage-self-audit.md`.

## Trigger

[client] describes a situation where progress is stalled, decisions aren't being made, or people are behaving in ways that don't make sense. Also triggers when [client] is trying to map organisational dynamics or understand resistance to a change initiative.

## Pattern Categories

### Decisions and Meetings

- **Death by committee.** Requiring decisions to pass through too many people. "Let's get alignment from..." when alignment isn't actually needed.
- **Endless speeches.** Long, unfocused contributions in meetings that consume time without advancing decisions.
- **Scope creep in discussion.** Raising tangentially related issues to prevent focus on the actual decision.
- **Wordsmithing.** Debating exact phrasing of documents when the substance is settled.
- **Reopening settled decisions.** Requesting reconsideration of resolved issues, especially under new framing.
- **Advocating excessive caution.** "We should be careful about..." as a mechanism to delay, not as genuine risk management.
- **Questioning jurisdiction.** "Is this really our decision to make?" as a delay tactic.

### Leadership and Management

- **Assigning critical work to weak people.** High-risk, high-visibility work given to the least capable team members.
- **Rewarding wrong performers.** Promotion or recognition going to people who don't deliver, demoralising those who do.
- **Procedure overhead.** Requiring multiple sign-offs for low-risk changes. Process as a substitute for trust.
- **Inflexible rules.** Applying policy rigidly when the spirit of the policy clearly doesn't apply.
- **Demanding written orders.** Requiring everything in writing as a mechanism to slow things down or create CYA paper trails.

### Communication and Administration

- **Misfiling or losing documents.** Critical information that can never be found when needed.
- **Spreading unverified information.** Rumours presented as facts, creating confusion and anxiety.
- **Extending correspondence.** Emails that generate more emails. Threads that never converge.
- **Hoarding information.** Key context held by one person and not shared until the last moment.

### Individual Productivity

- **Working slowly with good excuses.** Output that's consistently below what the person is capable of, with plausible explanations.
- **Blaming tools.** "The system is slow," "The process doesn't support this," with no effort to fix the tools.
- **Withholding expertise.** Knowing how to solve a problem but not offering to help.
- **Feigning confusion.** "I don't understand what's being asked" when the ask is clear.
- **Chronic rework.** Delivering work that needs to be redone, consuming reviewer time.

### Morale and Culture

- **Lengthy, incomprehensible explanations.** Making simple things sound complicated to maintain power through confusion.
- **Cold treatment.** Excluding people from conversations, meetings, or decisions without explicit reason.
- **Emotional outbursts.** Using anger or frustration to shut down legitimate challenge.
- **Chronic complaints without proposals.** Identifying problems constantly but never suggesting solutions.

## How Marty Uses This

Marty does not accuse people of deliberate sabotage. The point is: accidental sabotage looks identical to intentional sabotage, and the fix is the same regardless of intent. Marty names the *pattern*, not the *motive*.

"That sounds like a scope-creep-in-discussion pattern. The decision is X — is the tangent genuinely relevant, or is it preventing the decision?"

"You're describing information hoarding. Does [person] know you need this, or have they not been asked directly?"
