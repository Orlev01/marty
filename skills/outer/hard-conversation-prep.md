# Hard Conversation Prep

## Trigger

[client] has an upcoming conversation they're anticipating as difficult — performance feedback, negotiation, pushback to a senior stakeholder, conflict resolution, delivering bad news. Also triggers when [client] is avoiding a conversation they know they need to have.

## What This Skill Does

Prepares [client] to walk into the conversation with clarity about their goal, awareness of the other person's likely position, and specific tools for the moments that will be hardest.

## Preparation Framework

### 1. Define the Goal

"When this conversation is over, what needs to be true?"

Not a vague outcome ("I want them to understand"). A concrete state change ("They agree to allocate the platform engineer full-time by next week" or "I know whether the budget includes a security partner or not").

If [client] can't define the goal, the conversation isn't ready to have. Coach on the goal first.

### 2. Map the Other Person

Reference `people/` if a file exists. Key questions:
- What do they want from this conversation?
- What are they afraid of?
- What pressures are they under that [client] might not see?
- What's the best possible outcome for them?

### 3. Accusation Audit

List every negative thing the other person might think or feel about [client]'s position, [client]'s ask, or [client]'s behaviour. This is preparation, not a script — [client] may use some of these labels at the start of the conversation to defuse them.

"You're probably thinking I'm pushing back on something you've already decided."
"You might feel like I'm not being a team player here."
"This might come across as me not trusting the team."

### 4. Prepare Calibrated Questions

2-3 open-ended questions that steer the conversation toward [client]'s goal without making demands. "How" and "What" questions, never "Why."

"How do you see the timeline working with the current resourcing?"
"What would need to change for this to be feasible?"
"How should we handle it if [risk] materialises?"

### 5. Identify the Hard Moment

Every hard conversation has a specific moment that's hardest — the ask, the pushback, the silence after delivering bad news. Name it in advance. Plan what [client] does in that moment (usually: say the thing, then stop talking).

### 6. Define the Walk-Away

What's [client]'s position if the conversation doesn't go well? What's the fallback? What's the escalation path? Knowing this before walking in changes how [client] carries themselves in the room.

## Connection to Values

Links to **Honesty and directness** — hard conversations are the implementation of this value. Avoiding them is a violation of it.

Links to **Firm but kind, band loyalty** — the tone of the hard conversation. Direct truth, delivered with respect, in service of the relationship and the band.
